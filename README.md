# 🛡️ **Nexus Financial Services GRC Assessment**

This portfolio project shows how I translated **three governance, risk and compliance gaps** at a fictional financial services company into **one integrated recommendation**. I assessed the potential business impact, proposed owners for key controls and identified what evidence management could use to review them.

> **Skills demonstrated:** Control gap analysis · Business risk assessment · Compliance accountability · Control design · Management reporting

## 🏢 **Business Context**

The scenario describes Nexus Financial Services as a growing company in Lagos, Nigeria, with approximately US$800 million in assets. Its GRC processes have remained informal as it has grown. The assessment examines three points where that informality matters: approving and enforcing policies, evaluating risks before business decisions, and tracking regulatory obligations.

## 🔎 **Assessment Approach**

1. Identify the gaps stated in the scenario and classify each under its primary GRC area.
2. Assess how each gap could affect decisions, accountability or regulatory follow-through.
3. Map the proposed responsibilities across business owners, risk and compliance oversight, and independent audit assurance.
4. Define the evidence management could review after implementation.

## 📋 **Key Findings**

| ID | GRC area | Scenario observation | Potential business impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `G-01` | **Governance** | Nexus has no clear process for approving or enforcing company policies. | Teams may apply policies inconsistently, and managers may have no clear route for resolving or escalating exceptions. |
| `R-02` | **Risk management** | Nexus has no standard approach to evaluating risk before business decisions. | Managers may assess similar proposals differently or approve decisions without understanding the exposure they are accepting. |
| `C-03` | **Compliance** | No one is designated to track regulatory requirements and deadlines. | Changes may go unnoticed, obligations may be missed, and overdue actions may be escalated too late. |

## 🎯 **Integrated Recommendation**

**Establish a proportionate, management-approved GRC framework.** Name one coordinator to oversee implementation and assign responsibility for each activity to the relevant operational owner.

- **Policy governance:** Define who owns, approves and implements each material policy, when it should be reviewed, and how exceptions should be escalated.
- **Risk assessment:** Require a documented assessment before material decisions. Record the likelihood and impact of identified risks, existing controls, the exposure that remains, and who has authority to accept it.
- **Regulatory compliance:** Maintain a register of applicable obligations with named owners, due dates, status, completion evidence and a route for escalating overdue actions.

Senior management should approve and resource the framework, review material exceptions and require corrective action. Operational teams should own and apply their controls. Designated risk and compliance personnel should coordinate, monitor and challenge, while internal audit can provide independent assurance where available.

## ✅ **Evidence of Control Effectiveness**

| Review area | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Policy use | Material policies have approved owners and review dates, and teams can show how they apply them and handle exceptions. |
| Risk-informed decisions | Assessments were completed before approval, address relevant exposures, and show how risk informed the decision. |
| Regulatory follow-through | Applicable obligations have owners and deadlines, with evidence of timely completion or escalation. |
| Corrective action | Management reviews overdue actions and recurring exceptions and checks whether corrective actions resolve the underlying problem. |

## 📌 **Scope and Evidence Limits**

Nexus Financial Services is fictional. This was a portfolio project, not a client engagement or an audit. The findings reflect the scenario as provided, and the impacts are potential consequences. The case does not establish which licences or specific obligations apply, whether incidents or breaches occurred, or whether other controls were effective. The proposed framework was not implemented or tested.
